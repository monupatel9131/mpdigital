How It Works

Understanding Your Speed Test

Each measurement represents a different part of your internet connection.

Ping / Latency

Measures how long a small request takes to receive a response. Lower latency is generally better.

Download Speed

Measures how quickly data can be received by your browser from the test service.

Upload Speed

Measures how quickly data can be sent from your browser to the test service.

Jitter

Shows variation between latency measurements. Lower jitter is generally desirable for calls and gaming.

Packet Loss

Browser tests can estimate request failures, but this is not equivalent to an ICMP packet-loss test.

Mbps

Mbps means megabits per second and is the unit used for network transfer speed.

Accuracy Tips

Get a More Accurate Speed Test

Your device and local network can significantly influence measured performance.

01

Use 5 GHz Wi-Fi when possible

5 GHz can provide higher throughput at shorter range compared with 2.4 GHz.

02

Move closer to your router

Distance and walls can reduce Wi-Fi performance and increase instability.

03

Pause downloads and streaming

Other active traffic competes for the same connection bandwidth.

04

Disconnect unnecessary devices

Other phones, TVs and computers can consume bandwidth in the background.

05

Use Ethernet for best local accuracy

A wired connection can remove much of the variability introduced by Wi-Fi.

06

Test at different times

ISP congestion and network conditions can change throughout the day.

07

Test more than once

A single measurement is only a snapshot of current network conditions.

08

Consider VPN effects

Disable your VPN for a normal connection test, or keep it enabled if you want to measure VPN performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Speed Test FAQ

Common questions about internet speed testing and the measurements shown here.

An internet speed test measures network performance by transferring data between your browser and a test server. MP Speed Test measures download throughput, upload throughput and latency-related metrics where the browser can reliably measure them.
Results are real browser measurements, but they are not laboratory or ISP-certified measurements. Wi-Fi conditions, device performance, browser behavior, server routing, congestion and other traffic can affect results.
Your advertised plan is only one factor. Wi-Fi signal, router capability, device limitations, network congestion, ISP routing and other active traffic can produce a different measured result.
Many internet plans are designed with higher download capacity than upload capacity. However, some fiber and business connections offer symmetrical speeds.
Ping, or latency, is the time required for a request to travel to the test service and receive a response. It is usually measured in milliseconds.
Jitter describes variation in latency between repeated measurements. Lower and more consistent latency is generally preferable for real-time communication and gaming.
Packet loss occurs when data does not successfully reach its destination. Browser-based HTTP tests cannot perform a complete ICMP diagnostic, so MP Speed Test only reports request-loss information when it can calculate it meaningfully.

Privacy Notice

Your Test Runs In Your Browser

MP Speed Test is designed as a static browser-based utility. Speed-test requests are initiated by your browser. No account is required, and the site does not intentionally send your test history to an MP Speed Test server.

When Network Information is requested, public IP information may be requested from an external service such as Cloudflare's metadata service. That external service may have its own privacy policies. Browser localStorage may be used for test history and theme preference.
